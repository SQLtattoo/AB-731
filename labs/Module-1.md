# Module 1 Lab: Foundations of Generative AI and Business Value

> **Estimated time:** ~30 minutes  
> **Audience:** Business leaders and decision-makers

## Learning objective

By the end of this lab, you will be able to identify potential generative AI use cases, evaluate basic model and cost considerations, recognize common adoption risks, strengthen prompts, explain how grounding improves trust, and distinguish machine learning from generative AI use cases.

## Overview

Generative AI is reshaping how organizations innovate, operate, and compete. Business leaders can unlock new opportunities and lead transformation when they understand the underlying concepts, technology choices, and adoption trade-offs.

In these activities, you will use Copilot to apply these concepts to your own team.

## Activity 1: Explore generative AI business value

### Step 1: Scan for business value

1. Open Copilot.
2. Enter the following prompt.
3. Review the suggested candidates and consider whether they match your team's priorities.

> **Prompt 1.1: Business value scan**
>
> Act as a business value analyst. Based on my team's top three recurring tasks, identify which are strong candidates for generative AI versus traditional automation, and explain why.

### Step 2: Evaluate model and cost considerations

Enter the following prompt and compare the trade-offs Copilot identifies.

> **Prompt 1.2: Model and cost evaluation**
>
> Compare a pretrained foundation model to a fine-tuned model for drafting customer proposals. Explain the cost drivers, including token usage, and the ROI considerations I should weigh before choosing one.

### Step 3: Identify potential risks

Replace the bracketed text with a business process that is relevant to your role, and then enter the prompt.

> **Prompt 1.3: Risk awareness**
>
> List the top three risks of using generative AI for [a business process I choose], including fabrications, reliability, and bias, and suggest one mitigation for each.

## Reflect

- Which of your team's workflows would benefit most from generative AI, and which would be a poor fit? Why?
- How would you explain token-based cost to your finance team in one sentence?

## Activity 2: Prompt engineering for business impact

> **Estimated time:** 7 minutes

Module 1 introduces prompt engineering because crafting effective prompts is one of the essential elements of building reliable, responsible AI solutions that deliver real business value. A strong prompt gives Copilot a clear goal, context, expectations, and source material—the same structure AB-731 tests under "Describe the impact of prompt engineering."

### Try it: Strengthen a prompt step by step

Start from a weak prompt such as "Write a status update," and then add detail across three tries.

> **Prompt 2.1: Add goal and context**
>
> I'm a business leader preparing a weekly status update for my leadership team on our AI adoption pilot. Summarize this week's progress in three bullet points.

Open the [fictional project tracker](assets/Fictional_Project_Tracker_Copilot_Lab.xlsx) and attach it to Copilot before entering Prompt 2.2.

> **Prompt 2.2: Add expectations and a grounded source**
>
> Using the attached project tracker, draft a 150-word executive summary of this week's AI pilot progress, written for a leadership audience, highlighting one risk and one win.

> **Prompt 2.3: Iterate**
>
> Make that summary more concise and add a one-line recommendation for next steps.

### Reflect

- What changed in Copilot's response as you added more context and constraints?
- Where in your role could one well-engineered prompt save you the most time this week?

## Activity 3: Grounding AI in trusted data and building trustworthy AI

> **Estimated time:** 6 minutes

The same module explains how grounding and retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) improve accuracy and trust, and why data quality and security are prerequisites for any AI solution leaders can rely on.

### Try it: Copilot prompts

> **Prompt 3.1: Grounding scenario**
>
> Explain how retrieval-augmented generation would let Copilot answer questions using our internal policy documents instead of general web knowledge. What business risk does grounding reduce?

For Prompt 3.2, attach the provided [fictional customer survey feedback](assets/Fictional_Customer_Survey_Feedback.xlsx), or substitute a dataset relevant to your role.

> **Prompt 3.2: Data quality check**
>
> I'm about to ground an AI solution in [a dataset I choose, such as customer feedback survey results]. What data quality and security questions should I ask before I trust its answers?

> **Prompt 3.3: Governance**
>
> Draft three questions I should bring to our AI council about data security and responsible AI before rolling out this solution to my team.

### Reflect

- What's one dataset in your organization you'd want an AI solution grounded in, and what would you need to verify about it first?

## Activity 4: Recognizing the business value of machine learning

> **Estimated time:** 4 minutes

Module 1 closes its core content by teaching you to recognize when machine learning adds value and to describe the lifecycle of a machine learning solution, a distinction AB-731 tests directly alongside generative AI use cases.

### Try it: Copilot prompts

> **Prompt 4.1: Generative AI or machine learning?**
>
> I want to predict which customers are likely to churn next quarter. Should this be a generative AI solution or a machine learning solution? Explain your reasoning and outline the key stages of the machine learning lifecycle I'd need to plan for.

> **Prompt 4.2: Sort your own scenarios**
>
> Give me two business scenarios in my industry where machine learning would add more value than generative AI, and two where the reverse is true.

### Reflect

- Which upcoming decision on your team hinges on choosing between generative AI and machine learning?

## Key takeaway

AI delivers the most business value when leaders choose the right approach for the use case, engineer clear prompts, ground solutions in trusted data, and evaluate cost, quality, security, and risk together.
