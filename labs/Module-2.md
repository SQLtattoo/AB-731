# Module 2 Lab: Microsoft AI apps, services, and adoption decisions

> **Estimated time:** ~30 minutes  
> **Audience:** Business leaders and decision-makers

This lab builds on the earlier AB-731 lab for exploring the business value of generative AI solutions and turns the two modules of *Drive business value with AI solutions* into a fast, hands-on 30-minute session focused on Microsoft's AI apps and services. You will use prompts in Copilot to map real Microsoft Copilot and Microsoft Foundry capabilities to your own organization and practice the licensing, extensibility, and governance decisions AB-731 tests under its "Identify benefits, capabilities, and opportunities" and "Identify an implementation and adoption strategy" skill domains.

Work through the four activities below in order, either on your own or with a facilitator. Each activity pairs a short concept recap with prompts you can run right now in Copilot, plus reflection questions that connect the ideas to your own role.

## Activity 1: Mapping Copilot to business processes

> **Estimated time:** 7 minutes

Module 1 shows how Copilot integrates with Microsoft 365 apps to automate routine tasks, deliver actionable insights, and support secure, compliant innovation across your organization. AB-731 tests this directly under "Map business processes and use cases to Copilot" and "Identify when to use Researcher or Analyst in Copilot."

### Try it: Copilot prompts

> **Prompt 1.1: Process mapping**
>
> List my team's five most time-consuming recurring tasks, and for each one recommend whether Microsoft 365 Copilot, Copilot Chat, or a Copilot Studio agent is the best fit, and why.

> **Prompt 1.2: Researcher vs. Analyst agents**
>
> Explain when I should use the Researcher agent versus the Analyst agent in Copilot, and give one example from my role as a business leader for each.

> **Prompt 1.3: Cross-app capability**
>
> Compare how Copilot's capabilities differ across Outlook, Excel, and Teams, and identify one workflow in my role where combining all three would save the most time.

### Reflect

- Which of your recurring tasks would you map to Copilot first, and why?
- Where might a Copilot Studio agent outperform a general Copilot prompt in your organization?

## Activity 2: Licensing, extensibility, and build, buy, or extend

> **Estimated time:** 6 minutes

The module also equips you to evaluate options for adopting, licensing, and extending AI solutions to fit your organization's needs, including when to build a custom agent, buy an existing solution, or extend Copilot through its extensibility framework—a decision AB-731 tests explicitly.

### Try it: Copilot prompts

> **Prompt 2.1: Build, buy, or extend**
>
> I need an AI solution for [a specific business need, such as automating vendor onboarding]. Help me decide whether to build a custom Copilot agent, buy an existing solution, or extend Microsoft 365 Copilot, and explain the trade-offs of each.

> **Prompt 2.2: Licensing options**
>
> Explain the differences between Microsoft 365 Copilot license types, including pay-as-you-go, monthly, and included-with-subscription options, and recommend which fits a team of [size] doing [task type].

### Reflect

- Which licensing model would best fit your team's current Copilot usage, and why?

## Activity 3: Matching business needs to Microsoft Foundry Tools

> **Estimated time:** 7 minutes

Module 2 explains how Microsoft Foundry Tools, such as Azure Vision, Azure Language, Azure Document Intelligence, and Azure AI Search, enable secure, compliant solutions for real business challenges. It also walks through approaches with prebuilt tools, Foundry Models, and retrieval-augmented generation (RAG). AB-731 tests this under "Identify capabilities of Foundry Tools" and "Match an AI model to a business need."

### Try it: Copilot prompts

> **Prompt 3.1: Tool matching**
>
> I need to extract data from scanned invoices, translate customer support tickets, and search a large document archive by meaning rather than keyword. Match each of these three needs to the Microsoft Foundry Tool best suited for it.

> **Prompt 3.2: Approach selection**
>
> For [a business scenario I choose], help me decide whether a prebuilt Foundry tool, a Foundry Model, or a retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) approach would deliver the most value, and explain the trade-offs.

### Reflect

- Which business challenge in your organization could a Foundry Tool solve today without custom development?

## Activity 4: Responsible AI governance and Foundry subscription models

> **Estimated time:** 4 minutes

Both modules close on adoption strategy: aligning AI investments with Microsoft's responsible AI policies, standing up governance such as an AI council, and choosing the right Foundry subscription model—all part of AB-731's implementation and adoption domain.

### Try it: Copilot prompts

> **Prompt 4.1: Responsible AI checklist**
>
> Draft a one-page checklist to help my team confirm an AI solution meets Microsoft's responsible AI standards, including fairness, reliability, safety, privacy, security, inclusiveness, transparency, and accountability, before we launch it.

> **Prompt 4.2: Adoption and subscription models**
>
> Compare Foundry Tools' pay-as-you-go and commitment-tier subscription models for [an estimated usage volume], and recommend which reduces cost risk for a first pilot.

### Reflect

- Who on your team could champion responsible AI practices, and what would you ask them to own first?
